# Screen Recording

A Notchcast add-on (`Tinycast/Features/Notchcast/ScreenRecording/`, switch `recording`, Settings › Notchcast › Capture, off by default). **Record Area**, **Record Window** and **Record Screen** record with ScreenCaptureKit, with system audio and optionally the microphone, to H.264 or HEVC in a `.mov` or `.mp4`. The recorder is adapted from Vorssaint's (GPL-3.0-or-later; each derived file carries its notice and a line in `notchcast/licenses/borrowed.md`). While it records, the island shows a stop control with the elapsed time; after stop a trimmer shows the clip with start and end handles and offers Save, Copy or Discard.

## Invariants

- **One recording at a time, decided in `RecordingState`.** Every command and callback goes through it (`Model/RecordingState.swift`): idle → selecting → starting → recording → stopping → trimming → idle. Any Record command while recording stops it, so one hotkey starts and stops; while picking, starting, stopping or trimming a command does nothing. Callbacks for an older recording (a late pick, a late start, a late trimmer answer) carry its operation id and stand down.
- **The island is reached only through `context.presenter`.** Recording sets one `IslandStatus` (id `recording`, `stop.circle`, the elapsed time as its text) whose tap stops, plus one pill-menu row "Stop Recording". The text fits the status's four characters: `m:ss` under ten minutes, then `NNm`, then `HhMM` (`RecordingClock`). Beside a notch that is all that shows; nothing else of the add-on draws on the island. Results are notices with id `recording`: "Saving Recording…" (untitled progress, compact on a notch), then "Recording Saved" (tap reveals the file in Finder), "Recording Copied", or a failure.
- **A recorded clip is never lost by the add-on.** The clip is written to a work folder (`Screen Recordings` in the temporary directory) as `<uuid>.mov` and leaves it only after the export succeeds. A recording folder that is missing is made again before the export. When the export still fails (the drive is gone, the encoder refuses), the clip moves whole to the Desktop under a free name and the failure notice says so; its tap reveals the file (`RecordingRescue`). Switching the add-on off while recording or trimming stops the capture and saves the clip whole, untrimmed, to the recording folder. A quit while recording or trimming leaves the clip in the work folder: `ScreenRecordingRecovery` (the add-on's `makeRecovery`) runs at every core start, whatever the switch says, and moves each `<uuid>.mov` it finds there to the recording folder, or the Desktop when that fails, with one notice "Recovered a recording from the last session." whose tap reveals it. A copied export also lives in the work folder under its file name and is left alone. Only Discard deletes a clip. The trimmer has no click-away or Esc close for the same reason.
- **Trimming passes through where it can.** The recorded clip is always a MOV. `RecordingExport.plan` keeps an untrimmed MOV as recorded (a move), cuts or rewraps into MP4 with `AVAssetExportPresetPassthrough` when `AVAssetExportSession.compatibility` allows it for the container, and re-encodes at `AVAssetExportPresetHighestQuality` only otherwise. An export is staged as a hidden sibling of its destination and renamed into place, so a failed one leaves nothing under the final name (`RecordingExporter`). Handles never cross and keep at least `RecordingTrim.minimumLength` (0.2 s).
- **Capture and file are separate, on one serial queue.** `RecordingCaptureEngine` owns the `SCStream` and nothing else: it drops idle and blank frames, moves each buffer onto the host clock and hands it over. `RecordingWriter` is the `AVAssetWriter`: the time the capture was asked to start is the file's zero, the first image is held over startup latency, the last one is written again at the stop so a still screen keeps its length, and the file is fragmented so a crash leaves it readable. HEVC is used only when one test frame encodes at that size, else H.264. System audio and the microphone (`RecordingMicrophone`, the default input through `AVCaptureSession`, retimed onto the same clock) are separate AAC tracks. `RecordingSession` starts the writer, the stream, then the microphone, and stops in the order stream, microphone, drain, finish. `RecordingCaptureLifecycle` rejects every callback once a stop began.
- **What is recorded.** The pointer shows. Our own process's audio is excluded. A window is recorded on its own, scaled into the size it started with. Whether the island shows in a recording follows Island › "Show in screenshots and videos", like Screenshots: no window of ours is excluded by the add-on. A capture that stops by itself (its window closed, sharing stopped from the menu bar) goes to the trimmer with what it recorded, also when it stops while it is still starting. So does one cut short by the Mac going to sleep or by a switch to another user: `ScreenRecordingCapture` listens for both while it records.
- **Disk and sleep.** A recording starts only with 2 GB free and stops itself under 500 MB (`RecordingDisk`), keeping the clip for the trimmer and saying so; free space is read off the main actor once a second. Idle sleep is held off while it records.
- **Two surfaces of its own, both outside the island** (design-system.md §12 lets an add-on open none; these are the issue's own): the selection surface, a shielding-level dimming panel per display like Screenshots' (drag an area; or click the highlighted window; Esc or a right click cancels), and the trimmer, upstream's floating-panel recipe (`CameraPanel`/`CameraView`: `panelScrim` over `GlassEffectView`, `Theme.Radius.dialog`, `Theme.Size.cameraStage` for the video, `.modalAction` buttons; Return saves). Its bar is a filmstrip of 14 frames with the cut ends dimmed and a handle at each end.
- **Permissions are asked from the command.** Screen Recording through `CGRequestScreenCaptureAccess` before the picker opens; Microphone, only with "Record microphone" on, before capture starts. A refusal is a failure notice and records nothing; so is a microphone that will not start.

## Settings

Under the switch: Record system audio (on), Record microphone (off), Video codec (H.264, HEVC), File format (MOV, MP4), Recording folder (Desktop until chosen). Stored in the `.notchcast` suite under `notchcast.recording.*`; none is carried by `settings.json`. Files are named as macOS names its own, `Screen Recording <yyyy-MM-dd> at <HH.mm.ss>.<ext>`, counting up on a clash. Copy writes the trimmed file into the work folder and puts its file URL on the clipboard.

## Proof

- `Tests/nc-ScreenRecording-model-test.swift`: every state transition, the clock text, trim clamping and the 10 s → 2…8 s → 6 s case, the export plan per container, the filmstrip times, window picking and area geometry, pixel sizes, the bit rate, the disk floors, the capture lifecycle, options and file names.
- `Tests/nc-ScreenRecording-module-test.swift`: the shipped descriptor and module with a fake system, clock and presenter (no capture, no sleeps): record a window, the status ticking 0:00 → 0:10, the sleep hold, stop from the status, the menu row and a second command, the trimmer, Save cut to 2…8 s by passthrough into the Desktop, Copy re-encoding into the clipboard, Discard, an uncut MP4, name clashes, permission, microphone and disk refusals, a disk filling up, a capture ending by itself and while it starts, an empty recording, a failed export moving the clip whole to the Desktop, a deleted recording folder made again, switch-off while picking, recording and trimming, and the recovery at start moving a clip the last run left behind.
- `Tests/nc-ScreenRecording-writer-test.swift`: the real writer and exporter fed 10 s of generated frames and sound, with no capture: a 10 s MOV with picture and sound, cut to 2…8 s into a 6 s file with both, an uncut MP4, a failed export leaving nothing staged, a frameless recording, a still screen held to its stop, retiming and interleaving. It also compiles the capture engine, microphone and session, which it never starts.

Not verified by a harness: the live ScreenCaptureKit capture and microphone, the stop on sleep and on a user switch, the saved file playing with sound in a player, the selection surface and trimmer on screen.

With the microphone on, a file has two sound tracks (system, microphone); QuickTime plays both, some players only the first.
